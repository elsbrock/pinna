# Pinna 0.7.0

Pinna runs on the Echo Dot (5th gen), and the installer in your browser installs it. System has a Timeline page that shows what the satellite does. Satellites in Home Assistant mode take messages and control from their satellite group.

## Before you update

- The Echo Dot (2nd gen) and the Echo (2nd gen) get a rebuilt kernel and a
  root image that is compressed differently. If the new version doesn't
  start, the satellite goes back to the previous one by itself. To go back by
  hand, see
  [Go back to an older version](https://getpinna.dev/docs/update/#go-back-to-an-older-version).
- **A satellite in Home Assistant mode now takes messages and control from
  its satellite group.** Before, it took none. If you don't want that, turn
  off **Group messages** and **Control from the group** under
  **Assistant** › **Satellite group** on its dashboard.
- The image no longer has `vim`, `evtest`, the `openssl` command, Perl, or
  BlueZ's `btmon`, `gatttool`, `sdptool`, `obexctl` and `mpris-proxy`.
  Nothing on the dashboard uses them; this matters only if you work on the
  satellite over SSH.

## Highlights

### The Echo Dot (5th gen)

Pinna runs on the Echo Dot (5th gen), model C2N6L4. It doesn't run on the
Echo Dot with clock (5th gen) or the Echo Pop. The ring, the buttons, the
microphone switch and the speaker work. The satellite hears through all three
microphones with its own sound removed, and the wake word and requests
through Home Assistant work.
Updates install over the air, as on the other Echos.

First unlock the device with
[firebreak](https://github.com/Gamer92000/firebreak), which needs wires
soldered to the USB test pads under its foot. Then start TWRP with **Volume
Up**, connect the test pads' USB, and open the
[installer](https://getpinna.dev/install/) in your browser. It checks the
device, writes `boot_a` and `system_a`, starts slot A on trial, and restarts.
TWRP stays in slot B until the first update. The installer downloads this
release, and checks the tool it runs in TWRP, `pinna-bootctl`, against the
release's signature. `install-echo-dot-5.sh` on your computer does the same
over adb. See
[Install on the Echo Dot (5th gen)](https://getpinna.dev/docs/flash/#install-on-the-echo-dot-5th-gen),
and [Devices](https://getpinna.dev/docs/devices/#echo-dot-5th-gen) for what
works.

### A timeline of what the satellite does

**System** › **Timeline** turns debugging on for 15 minutes, 1 hour or 8
hours. It draws the satellite's phases, wake words and near misses, each
stage of a request, and every request it sends to a service, on one time
axis, and lists the requests in a table. With a dashboard password it also
draws the audio levels: the microphones before and after the gain, each wake
word's score, and the speaker. The text of the requests needs a press of
**Action** on the satellite as well. The satellite keeps the last 10
minutes; **Download** saves them as a file for an issue.

Nothing is recorded until you start it, and **Stop** clears what it kept.
While it runs, **Overview** says so. See
[Follow a request on the timeline](https://getpinna.dev/docs/troubleshooting/#follow-a-request-on-the-timeline).

### Satellites in Home Assistant mode join in the group

A satellite in Home Assistant mode now takes messages from the other members
of its satellite group, and runs what they ask of it: the volume, the reply
volume, the ring, music and radio. It refuses timers, because Home Assistant
sets its timers. It speaks messages with its own text-to-speech service
under **Assistant** › **Services**, which has to pass its check. It sends no
messages itself: you still ask a satellite in standalone mode. See
[Satellite groups](https://getpinna.dev/docs/satellite-groups/).

The satellites of a group also find each other more reliably. The search
asks three times, and keeps a satellite listed while its dashboard still
answers.

### Open an issue, Help and a theme switch

On **System** › **Logs**, **Open an issue** takes the place of Copy for an
issue. It opens GitHub's issue form in a new tab with the Satellite field
filled in, and copies the log for you to paste into its Log field. Issues are
public, so read the log before you post it.

**System** has a Help group: Report a problem, Ask a question, Share feedback
and Read the docs. The sidebar has a Feedback link. A new discussion carries
one line with the version, the device and the mode, and nothing that names
you or your network.

A button next to the logo at the top of the sidebar switches the dashboard
between the System, Light and Dark themes. On a phone, the switch is under
**System** › **This browser** › **Theme**. The choice is kept in your
browser, not on the satellite.

## New in Home Assistant

- **Debugging** (binary sensor): on while debugging runs on the satellite.
  You can only read it; debugging starts and stops on the dashboard.

## Other changes

- The device box in the sidebar opens into the list of satellites. Each
  satellite shows its drawing and its name only.
- **System** › **Logs** keeps the width of the other pages. A line names its
  level only when it is a warning or an error, and those lines stand out.
  Copy and Download are icon buttons.
- The root image is smaller, so an update downloads less.

## Fixed

- The Echo (2nd gen) plays its woofer's range from 40 Hz. The speaker took
  away everything under 150 Hz, which suits only the Echo Dot's small
  driver.
- The Echo Dot (2nd gen) and the Echo (2nd gen) keep receiving broadcasts
  on Wi-Fi after the access point changes its group key. Before, an access
  point that rekeys often, such as a FRITZ!Box, could cut off DHCP, ARP and
  mDNS until the satellite rejoined.
- The wake word is caught more easily while the satellite plays music or
  speaks.
- Satellites of one group list each other reliably. Before, a satellite
  could miss another one for minutes.
- **Copy** on the Logs page says when the browser didn't copy, and points to
  Download, instead of reporting a copy that didn't happen.
- On a phone, the Logs page keeps the newest line in view.
- A restart no longer waits 90 seconds for the login on the USB serial
  console to end.
- The log no longer fills with "bluetooth: running with the Wi-Fi up" every
  31 seconds while Bluetooth runs.
- Starting Bluetooth no longer logs a systemctl failure under AirPlay's name
  on every boot.
- A restart no longer logs that the AirPlay receiver or the Bluetooth bridge
  stopped.
