# Pinna 0.6.0-beta.1

Saying the wake word while the satellite talks now interrupts it more often: the wake word hears a stream of its own, with its own echo cancellation. The echo cancellation's reference no longer arrives late, and the microphones are louder.

## Before you update

This is a beta: it goes to satellites whose **Updates** setting is `beta`.

- **The wake word hears a new stream.** On the Echo Dot (2nd gen) and the
  Echo (2nd gen), the wake word detection listens to a stream with its own
  echo canceller. It was measured by replaying recordings from an Echo Dot
  (2nd gen), and hasn't run on an Echo yet. If that stream delivers nothing
  for a second, the satellite goes back to the old one by itself. If the wake
  word stops answering, or wakes when nobody said it, go back to 0.5.0, as
  [Go back to an older version](https://getpinna.dev/docs/update/#go-back-to-an-older-version)
  describes.

## Highlights

### Interrupt a reply with the wake word

The echo cancellation that Home Assistant and standalone requests hear also
mutes a person who talks while the satellite plays. The wake word detection
now hears a stream of its own instead: the same microphones, with a simpler
echo canceller that removes the satellite's own sound and leaves your voice.
In recordings from an Echo Dot (2nd gen) with a talker 1 m away, the wake
word was detected over a spoken reply 5, 2 and 4 times out of 5 at volumes 50,
60 and 70, against 0, 0 and 1 before, and the reply alone didn't wake it at
volumes 50 to 80. Requests still use the echo-cancelled stream. See [Microphones](https://getpinna.dev/docs/audio/#microphones).

## Other changes

- The microphones are routed at 0 dB instead of -6 dB. On an Echo Dot (2nd
  gen), every microphone got 5.5 to 6.2 dB louder.
- The root image carries the Wi-Fi firmware of the Echo Dot (3rd gen), and
  the kernel for it is built. Pinna doesn't run on that device yet.

## Fixed

- The echo cancellation's reference, the copy of what the satellite plays,
  arrived about 2 seconds after its echo, so it cancelled nothing. It now
  arrives before it. Measured on an Echo Dot (2nd gen) at volume 70: 11 dB
  cancelled over a spoken reply, and 35 to 48 dB while nobody talks.
- After PipeWire restarted, the echo cancellation had no reference until
  `pinnad` restarted. The reference now starts again by itself.
- One of the seven microphones could be switched off after a start. The
  satellite now checks the microphone settings once it records, and sets
  again any that don't hold.
- On a phone, the dashboard's top bar shows the mark without the live ring
  beside it.

## All changes

Changes since 0.5.0.

### New

- An arm64 kernel runs the armhf image as 32-bit programs, checked under QEMU (kernel)
- The MT8516 kernel, with the Echo Dot (3rd gen) board (kernel)
- MT7668 Wi-Fi for the MT8516 Echos (kernel)
- LK's 64-bit layout for the MT8516 Echos (pinna-bootimg)
- Firmware comes from a folder per board and Fire OS release, the Echo Dot (3rd gen)'s too (scripts)
- The root carries the Echo Dot (3rd gen)'s Wi-Fi firmware (image)
- A linear echo canceller for the wake word (pinna-audio)
- The pinna_wake plugin, the wake word's own stream (pinna-ladspa)
- The wake word detector hears its own stream on the Echo Dot (pinnad)
- The Echo Dot graph gets the wake word's stream, dot_wake (image)

### Fixed

- The MT7668 Wi-Fi driver reads its configuration as wifi_mt7668.cfg (kernel)
- The phone's top bar shows the mark without the live ring beside it (dashboard)
- The echo reference reaches the canceller before its echo (pinna-audio)
- Route the Echo microphones at 0 dB instead of -6 dB (pinna-audio)
- The echo reference starts pw-play again when it exits (pinna-audio)
- `pinnad` checks the microphone settings once capture runs (pinna-audio)
- The end of speech keeps its noise seed from dot_mic_aec (pinnad)
