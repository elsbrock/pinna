# Pinna 0.6.0-beta.4

The satellite hears you through one stream: all seven microphones averaged, with its own sound removed. Talking over music no longer throws the echo cancellation off, and what a track pans left now plays.

## Before you update

This is a beta: it goes to satellites whose **Updates** setting is `beta`.

- **The new microphone stream hasn't run on a satellite yet.** It was measured
  on recordings from an Echo Dot (2nd gen) and an Echo (2nd gen), played
  through the same code on a computer. If the wake word or your requests work
  worse after the update, switch back to 0.6.0-beta.3 under **System** ›
  **Updates**: see
  [Switch to the other version](https://getpinna.dev/docs/update/#switch-to-the-other-version).
- **Home Assistant hears a different stream.** Its requests used WebRTC's echo
  canceller, which also muted you while the satellite played. They now use the
  same stream as the wake word.

## Highlights

### One microphone stream, from all seven microphones

The satellite ran two microphone paths: a beamformer with WebRTC's echo
canceller for requests, and an average of four microphones with a linear echo
canceller for the wake word. Now one stream averages all seven microphones and
removes the satellite's own sound with the linear canceller. The wake word,
Home Assistant and your speech-to-text service all hear it. On recordings, a
person talking in a quiet room stood about 2 dB further above the room on
seven microphones than on four. The ring still points at the person speaking.

If the stream delivers nothing for a second, the satellite averages the raw
microphones itself, without echo cancellation, and tries the stream again
every minute. **System** › **Logs** then shows
`microphone: the processed stream delivers nothing`. See
[Microphones](https://getpinna.dev/docs/audio/#microphones).

### Echo cancellation that keeps up with music

The speaker of both Echos plays only the right playback channel, but the echo
canceller took the left one as its reference. From that one, a linear filter
removes about 1 dB of the speaker's echo; from the right one, 12.5 dB on the
Echo Dot (2nd gen) and 13.3 dB on the Echo (2nd gen). The canceller now takes
the speaker's channel.

It also adapts in smaller steps. On recordings from an Echo Dot (2nd gen), a
person talking over music threw it off: for the next 30 seconds it put out 7 to
13 dB more than the music it should remove. It now removes 22 to 24 dB. The cost is that it takes some seconds longer to adapt
to new audio.

### Stereo music plays in full

Because the speaker plays only the right channel, whatever a track panned left
never sounded. The satellite now mixes stereo audio to mono before it plays it.

## Other changes

- The echo reference comes from the microphone interface, so the satellite no
  longer plays a second copy of its audio for the echo canceller.
- **System** › **Record audio** records 11 channels: the seven microphones,
  the left and right playback channels, the stream before its automatic gain,
  and the echo reference.

## All changes

Changes since 0.6.0-beta.3.

### New

- The linear echo canceller takes a step size (pinna-audio)
- Micbench compares microphone front ends and echo cancellers offline (pinna-wakeword)
- A board says which channel its speaker plays and what its loopback lanes carry (pinna-board)
- Pinna_wake averages all the board's microphones and cancels from the speaker's lane (pinna-ladspa)
- The Echos' microphone graph is one stream, dot_wake (image)
- One microphone stream for the wake word and requests, with the raw lanes as fallback (pinnad)
- The bench tools follow pinna_wake's microphones and reference lane (pinna-wakeword)

### Fixed

- The speaker no longer sends its own echo reference (pinna-audio)
- The Echos play a mono mix, so stereo music keeps its left channel (pinna-audio)
- Talking over music no longer derails the wake word's echo canceller (pinna-audio)
